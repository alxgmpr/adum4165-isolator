## Overview

A galvanically isolated 4-port USB 2.0 hub. A USB-C host port (J1) feeds an **ADuM4165BRIZ** 5.7 kV<sub>rms</sub> high-speed USB isolator (U1, V<sub>ISO</sub> = 5700 V rms for 1 minute, V<sub>IORM</sub> = 849 V peak) with a local 24 MHz crystal on Side 1. The power barrier is set by T1's 5000 V rms insulation test voltage, so the assembly is a 5 kV<sub>rms</sub>-class design overall even though the isolator itself is rated higher. Isolated power is generated by an **SN6505BDBVR** push-pull driver (U5) into a **Würth 750313638** 1:1.3 transformer (T1), Schottky-rectified (D1/D2) to `DCDC_RAW` and regulated to 5.0 V by a **MIC29302WU** adjustable LDO (U6). An alternative external supply arrives on a power-only USB-C receptacle (J2); two **TLV7041DBVR** comparators (U13/U14) watch the CC lines for a 3 A source advertisement, and a **TPS2121RUXR** priority mux (U7) merges the two 5 V sources into `ISO_5V`. A **USB2514B-AEZC** 4-port hub (U4) runs from `ISO_3V3` (AP2112K-3.3, U8) with its own 24 MHz crystal, driving two USB-A (J3/J4) and two USB-C (J5/J6) downstream ports, each gated by a **TPS2553DBVR** current-limited switch (U9–U12) and protected by an **NUP4202W1T2G** ESD array (U15–U18).

Two ground domains only — `GND1` (host) and `GND2` (isolated) — bridged solely by U1 and T1. Design intent auto-classified as prototype / IPC class 2 / hobby (`design_intent.confidence` 0.3, all fields `auto`).

### DR-01 — the bus-powered ceiling is the host port, not the magnetics

The binding constraint in bus-powered mode is the **500 mA a USB 2.0 host port is obliged to supply**, and the arithmetic is short:

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Host budget, 500 mA at 5 V | 2.50 W |
| less U1 Side 1 (`IDD1(H)` 70 mA at 5 V) | −0.35 W |
| available to the converter | 2.15 W |
| × ~90 % end-to-end efficiency (SN6505B + transformer + rectifier) | 1.94 W |
| at `DCDC_RAW` ≈ 6.15 V → current through the LDO pass element | ≈ 315 mA |
| less MIC29302 ground current | **`ISO_5V` ceiling ≈ 310 mA** |
| less isolated-side self-draw | −235 mA |
| **left for all four downstream ports combined** | **≈ 75 mA** |

That is the real finding, and it is a **budget** fact, not a component-rating fact. Sensitivity: my own DCR-based estimate of `DCDC_RAW` lands between 5.8 V and 6.2 V depending on the SS34 forward drop, which moves the ceiling to ≈310–325 mA — it does not move the conclusion.

**The magnetics are not the limit, and the "100 mA" in TI's Table 9-3 is not a rating.** Four independent pieces of evidence:

1. The "100 mA" sits in Table 9-3's **application** column ("5 V → 5 V, 100 mA, SN6505B"), alongside a turns ratio and a size — it describes the operating point TI characterised, not a maximum.
2. TI's own curves for the electrically identical entry — Figures 6-21/6-22, captioned "SN6505B + Würth 760390014, VCC = 5 V", a 1:1.3, 5 V → 5 V, "100 mA" row — **sweep the load-current axis to 200 mA** with efficiency plotted to 90 %. TI characterises a "100 mA" entry at twice that current. (750313638's own rows carry **no** "Refer to Figure" reference, so TI published no curves for it at all.)
3. **The same part number appears twice in Table 9-3** with two different "ratings" — 1:1.3 for 5 V → 5 V at "100 mA" and 1.3:1 for 5 V → 3.3 V at "100 mA". A single transformer cannot have two different current ratings; the column is describing applications.
4. The LDO column is a **headroom** fact, not a capacity one, and TI corrected it after publication — the revision history records "Changed LDO from 'No' to 'Yes' for transformer ORDER NO. 750313638 and 750313626 in Table 9-3". It says post-regulation is needed, which this design already has.

Würth's own data agrees the part has headroom: the datasheet's "Typical Temperature Rise vs. Current Characteristics" curve (p.3) runs the **push-pull output current axis to 1.2 A**, with roughly a 40 K rise at 0.65 A — the same 0.65 A the agency-rated typical-application tables cite as I<sub>Out1</sub>. And the winding resistances hold the raw rail up: with R<sub>DC1</sub> = 0.35 Ω, R<sub>DC2</sub> = 0.33 Ω and the SN6505B's 0.25 Ω R<sub>ON</sub>, `DCDC_RAW` still sits at **≥ 5.49 V at 635 mA** — above the MIC29302's dropout at that current, so the 5 V rail would hold even at a load far beyond anything the host can feed.

Two further constraints compound the host-budget ceiling and belong on the bring-up sheet:

- A bus-powered USB device may draw **100 mA before configuration**. Side 1's draw during enumeration (U1 at 70 mA plus whatever the DC-DC reflects) exceeds that. The spec already accepts descriptor-honesty deviations for bus-powered mode; this is the electrical corollary.
- `ISO_3V3`'s own consumer, the hub, is 155 mA of the 235 mA self-draw — so most of the bus-powered budget is spent before a single downstream port is energised.

None of this is a wiring defect, and none of it argues for changing T1. "Bus-powered mode is a light-load mode" (spec, Known limitations) is the operative truth; what needed fixing was the spec's arithmetic, which has been corrected in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-25-usb-isolator-design.md`.

## Power Sequencing

Two regulators with controlled enables, both intentional and both verified against their datasheets:

| Regulator | Rail | EN net | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| U6 MIC29302WU | `DCDC_5V` | `DCDC_RAW` | EN tied to VIN — always enabled as soon as the rectifier has output. |
| U8 AP2112K-3.3 | `ISO_3V3` | `ISO_5V` | EN tied to VIN — `ISO_3V3` follows `ISO_5V`, so the hub cannot run before the isolated 5 V rail is up. |

U1's `PGOOD` (Side 2) drives `PGOOD2`, which feeds U4's `VBUS_DET` and the PGOOD LED buffer Q2. Per the ADuM4165 datasheet (p.22) PGOOD asserts only once **both** side supplies are above UVLO **and** a valid clock is present on XI1 — so `VBUS_DET` is a genuine "the host side is alive and clocked" signal, which is exactly what the hub needs to gate enumeration. Either side may power up first; when Side 2 comes up first it sits in low-power mode until Side 1 arrives.

**Carried decision (from Task 7):** ship `R25` only (PGOOD pull-down). If `PGOOD2` will not reach > 2.0 V with both sides up, PGOOD is open-drain — populate `R26` (pull-up) and remove `R25`. Both pads exist.

`HUB_RESET` is an RC (R16 10 k to `ISO_3V3`, C34 1 µF, τ ≈ 10 ms) with D5 as the discharge diode, so U4 releases reset well after `ISO_3V3` settles.

Per-IC comparison of actual usage against datasheet requirements, recorded in `analysis/deep_review.json` and validated by `deep_review_gate.py`: **14 findings, 14 verified, 0 partial, 0 quarantined.** Every finding carries at least one design anchor and at least one evidence source; every datasheet quote was re-extracted from the cited page of the local PDF by the gate itself.

### U1 — ADuM4165BRIZ

- **DR-07 (verified).** `VDD1`/`VDD2` bypass is **exactly 0.1 µF** (C5, C36). The datasheet (p.22) warns that more than 0.1 µF on these internal-LDO outputs can disrupt start-up — this is a value that must not be "improved" at layout. `VBUS1` (C4) and `VBUS2` (C35) also carry the specified 0.1 µF, and both supplies sit inside the 4.5–5.5 V window given in the pin table (p.12). *Fresh check this pass.*
- **DR-08 (verified).** Crystal requirement is "≤ 50 ppm tolerance with ≤ 100 ppm stability" (p.22) plus "a typical crystal capacitance of 10 pF and capacitive loads < 10 pF". C1/C2 are 8 pF C0G, satisfying the load constraint. `EVAL-ADuM4165EBZ` Table 4 pairs exactly those 8 pF loads with a 24 MHz / ±50 ppm / **8 pF** / 50 Ω 4-SMD crystal (IQD `LFXTAL056140REEL`) — so CL = 8 pF is ADI's own validated point, and that is what has been specified. ADI publishes no XI1/XO1 pin capacitance, so CL<sub>eff</sub> can only be bounded: 5.0 pF (2 pF stray) to 8.0 pF (8 pF stray), a pull error of at most **+71 ppm** at the low end.

- Pin map re-checked against p.12–13: VBUS1/GND1/VDD1/XI1/XO1/UD±/GND2/DD±/PGOOD/VDD2/VBUS2 all on the intended nets. ### U7 — TPS2121RUXR

- **DR-13 (verified).** ILIM: Equation 2 (p.13) gives ILM = 65.2 / R<sub>ILIM</sub><sup>0.861</sup>, valid 18–100 kΩ. R12 = 35.7 k → **3.002 A**, with OCP fold-back at 2.4× that. Soft-start C20 = 0.1 µF → 780 V/s (p.24) → ~38 mA switchover inrush into the 48.6 µF on `ISO_5V`. `OV1`/`OV2` on `GND2` means the overvoltage comparators never trip — the intended "no OV protection" configuration per Table 9-3.
- **DR-06 (fixed).** The CP2 / VCOMP defect and its one-wire fix — see [Critical Findings](#critical-findings). Post-fix truth-table walk:

  | # | Condition | `n3A_DET` | CP2 | PR1 | Table 9-3 row | OUT |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 3 A source on J2, host up | low | < VREF | 1.60 V ≥ VREF | CP2=0, PR1=1 | **IN1** (external) ✔ |
  | 2 | Weak / Default-USB source on J2, host up | high (3.3 V) | ≥ VREF | ≈ 0 | CP2=1, PR1=0 | **IN2** (DC-DC) ✔ |
  | 3 | Nothing on J2 | high | ≥ VREF | 0 (`EXT_5V` = 0) | IN1 ≤ UV → Invalid Input | **IN2** ✔ |
  | 4 | DC-DC failed, 3 A source present | low | < VREF | ≥ VREF | CP2=0, PR1=1 | **IN1** ✔ |
  | 5 | **Cold start: external present, host absent** | ≈ 0 (see below) | < VREF | 1.599 V ≥ VREF | rows 3 **and** 7 both → IN1 | **IN1** ✔ |

  **Cold-start quadrant (row 5), the case CP2-to-a-mux-derived-rail makes non-obvious.** With the host unplugged, `ISO_3V3` is dead, so R9 is a pull-up to 0 V — a *deterministic* pull-down on `n3A_DET`, not a float, and CP2 is unambiguously below V<sub>REF</sub>. PR1 is unaffected because its divider (R10/R11) is fed from `EXT_5V`, not from the mux output: 5 V × 47 k / 147 k = **1.599 V**, above the 1.06 V threshold. Table 9-3 resolves this two ways that agree — row 3 (`CP2 < VREF, PR1 ≥ VREF → IN1`) and row 7 (IN2 ≤ UV, "Invalid Input" → IN1) — so the mux comes up on the external supply. Once `ISO_5V` energises U8 and `ISO_3V3` rises, R9 pulls `n3A_DET` up only if no 3 A advertisement is present; with a genuine 3 A source the comparators hold it low and nothing changes. Either way row 7 keeps OUT on IN1 until the DC-DC is actually alive. **Stable in both directions; no start-up race.**

  **New risk found while completing this analysis: CP2's pull-up rail sits downstream of the mux.** The path is `OUT → U8 → ISO_3V3 → R9 → CP2`, so CP2's logic level depends on the rail the mux is producing. In the specific combination of *weak brick present + host present + DC-DC unable to hold up its load*, that closes a loop: DC-DC sags → IN2 drops toward the 2.55 V UV falling threshold → mux hands over to IN1 → the weak brick now carries the whole board → if it in turn sags, IN2 recovers unloaded and the mux hands back. The only damping is the TPS2121's own IN2 UVLO hysteresis (2.65 V rising / 2.55 V falling); nothing in the CP2 path adds any. The result would be a slow relaxation oscillation — hunting between sources — rather than a hard failure. It cannot occur while the DC-DC is comfortably within budget, which after DR-01 is the expected case, and it cannot occur at all without an external supply attached. **Mitigation, only if bring-up shows the DC-DC is marginal:** re-derive CP2's pull-up from `EXT_5V` through a divider (`EXT_5V` is upstream of the mux, so the loop opens) rather than from `ISO_3V3`. Do not implement pre-emptively — it costs two parts and the present wiring is correct for every non-marginal case.

  **Q1 is on the lockout critical path.** The whole lockout depends on Q1 pulling `PR1_SENSE` below 1.04 V while sinking the ~34 µA the R10/R11 divider delivers, driven by a 3.3 V gate. That is an enormous margin for any 2N7002 (V<sub>GS(th)</sub> ≤ 2.5 V worst case, and at 34 µA the required R<sub>DS(on)</sub> is trivial), so there is no practical risk — but it should be named: **the specified MPN's parametrics are not verified against a local datasheet**. `datasheets/2N7002.pdf` is JCET's part, the BOM specifies onsemi `2N7002LT1G`; the SOT-23 pinout was checked and matches, V<sub>GS(th)</sub> and R<sub>DS(on)</sub> were not.

  Loading check: control-pin leakage is ±0.1 µA max, so R9's 100 kΩ pull-up drops at most 10 mV — `n3A_DET` high presents ~3.29 V to CP2, well above the 1.06 V V<sub>REF</sub> rising threshold, and the comparator's V<sub>OL</sub> is a few mV, well below the 0.99 V falling threshold.

### U9–U12 — TPS2553DBVR

- **DR-14 (verified).** The three current-limit equations (p.15) at R = 40.2 kΩ give **592 / 649 / 713 mA** min/nom/max, inside the recommended 15–232 kΩ window. The 592 mA worst-case minimum clears the 500 mA a USB 2.0 high-power port must deliver; the 713 mA maximum × 4 ports = 2.85 A stays under U7's 3.00 A. Independently reproduces the Task-8 numbers.

### Interacting pairs

- **`ISO_5V` shared rail (U1, U8, U9–U12):** no sequencing conflict — U8's EN is tied to `ISO_5V`, so `ISO_3V3` cannot lead it; the TPS2553 switches are held off by `PRTPWR*` until the hub enumerates. Combined load analysed in [Power Budget](#power-budget).

Previously triaged in Task 8; **re-confirmed.** A discrete VBUS TVS per downstream port is a reasonable *addition* at layout if the board is destined for formal ESD testing, but its absence is not a defect. |
| `protocol_compliance` i2c missing pull-ups | 2 issues | `HUB_NON_REM1`, `HUB_CFG_SEL0` | These are U4's SMBus/I²C pins used **as configuration straps** — no EEPROM or SMBus master is fitted, per the spec's "default strap configuration (no SMBus/EEPROM)". Treating them as a live bus is a naming-based misfire. Previously triaged in Task 7; **re-confirmed.** |
| `lib_symbol_mismatch` (ERC warning) | 7 | Q1/Q2 group, U7, others | Cosmetic cache-vs-library-table divergence, all seven electrically verified during Tasks 5–9. Not surfaced at `--severity-error`. **Accepted.** |

Reviewer overrides that are *not* false positives but deliberate design choices worth recording:

- **U7 `OV1`/`OV2` tied to `GND2`** — overvoltage protection deliberately disabled; both sources are hard 5 V rails already bounded by their own regulators.
- **`ILIM`/`SS` pins typed `input` in the project symbol library** though the datasheets mark them `O` — an intentional ERC convention from Task 3.
- **D3/D4/D6 share one green LED MPN.** Colour differentiation (e.g. amber for the external-active indicator) is a drop-in BOM substitution at the same footprint, and green/amber V<sub>f</sub> differences are absorbed by the series resistors.
- **ST LED (D4) runs at ~273 µA** through R13 + R14 — visibly dim by design constraint (R13 doubles as the ST pull-up). Task-6 deferred minor; unchanged.

## Bring-Up Checklist

Ordered so that each step gates the next. Items marked **[decision]** resolve an open design question.

1. **Power-on, external supply only.** Fit J2 with a known-good 3 A USB-C source. Confirm `EXT_5V` ≈ 5 V, `ISO_5V` ≈ 5 V, `ISO_3V3` = 3.3 V, and that the ST LED (D4) is **on**. Measure `PR1_SENSE` ≥ 1.06 V and `n3A_DET` ≈ 0 V.
2. **Weak-source lockout.** Swap to a Default-USB (non-3 A) source. `n3A_DET` should rise to ~3.3 V, `PR1_SENSE` should collapse to ~0 V, D4 should go **off**, and `ISO_5V` should now track `DCDC_5V`, not `EXT_5V`. This is the direct test of the DR-06 fix — if `ISO_5V` follows the brick instead, the CP2 rewire did not take.
   - **Then watch `ISO_5V` and `n3A_DET` on a slow scope sweep for at least 30 s** with the weak brick and the host both connected. Steady state is expected; periodic switching between sources is the hunting mode described in DR-06 (CP2's pull-up rail sits downstream of the mux). If it hunts, the DC-DC is marginal — fix the load first, and only then consider re-deriving CP2's pull-up from `EXT_5V`.
2b. **Cold start, external only.** Power J2 with the host unplugged. The mux must come up on IN1 with no chatter: `PR1_SENSE` ≈ 1.60 V (its divider is fed from `EXT_5V`, independent of the mux), `n3A_DET` held near 0 V by R9 into a dead `ISO_3V3`, D4 on. Then hot-plug the host and confirm the transition to steady state is monotonic.
3. **[decision] DR-02 — MIC29302 light load.** With the external supply active (so `DCDC_5V` is unloaded), measure `DCDC_5V`. If it exceeds **5.25 V**, populate the bleeder (≈680 Ω to `GND2`, 7.3 mA) or drop R36 to 470 Ω and rescale R3/R4 to 3.01 k / 1.00 k. Leave the bleeder pad DNP if the measurement is clean.
4. **DR-01 — characterize the bus-powered margin.** Bus-powered only (J2 removed). Measure `DCDC_RAW`, `DCDC_5V` and the `ISO_5V` current with the hub enumerated and no downstream devices, and measure the current drawn from `VBUS_HOST` at the same time. Expected: ~235 mA on `ISO_5V`, `DCDC_RAW` around 6 V, and roughly 300 mA at the host. This is a **characterization** step — confirm the ≈310 mA ceiling and the ≈75 mA left for downstream ports, and check the end-to-end efficiency against the ~90 % assumed. It is not a transformer decision: the magnetics have headroom to 0.65 A (Würth's own thermal curve runs to 1.2 A) and the host's 500 mA budget binds first. Then attach one low-power device (a serial adapter or debug probe) and confirm it enumerates and runs.
   - If the host current approaches 500 mA with nothing attached, look at efficiency and at the hub's actual `ISO_3V3` draw before suspecting T1.
5. **[decision] PGOOD strap (carried from Task 7).** Ship `R25` only (pull-down). With both sides powered, measure `PGOOD2`. If it will not reach **> 2.0 V**, the ADuM's PGOOD is open-drain — populate `R26` (pull-up) and remove `R25`. Confirm U4 leaves reset and the PGOOD LED (D3, via Q2) lights.
6. **Clock verification and CL re-derivation.** Scope `XO1` (U1 pin 6) and `HUB_XO` (U4 pin 32) with a low-capacitance probe. Both must be 24 MHz. Measure the frequency error:
   - Y1 target CL is 8 pF against 8 pF loads; the predicted error spans 0 to **+71 ppm** depending on the ADuM's unpublished pin capacitance. If the measured error exceeds ADI's 50 ppm tolerance budget, raise C1/C2 — but stay **below 10 pF** (datasheet constraint).
   - Y2 target CL is 12 pF against 18 pF loads, predicted **−15 ppm**. If it is off, Microchip's formula C1 = 2 × (CL − C0) − CS1 with CXTAL = 6 pF gives the correction directly.
   - Also confirm the ADuM's 0.3 ms crystal start-up requirement is met out of suspend/resume.
7. **ILIM verification.** Per port, sweep an electronic load on `Px_VBUS` and record the trip current. Expect **592–713 mA** (649 mA nominal). Any port outside that band means R27–R30 is not a genuine ±1 % part or the ILIM trace is picking up noise (the datasheet warns the ILIM routing must be short). Then short one port and confirm (a) the corresponding `nOCSx` asserts, (b) the hub reports the fault upstream, and (c) the **other three ports stay up**.
8. **Mux current limit.** Confirm U7 limits at ~3.0 A, not lower — this is the ceiling under which all four ports at 713 mA (2.85 A) must fit.
9. **LDO thermal (DR-05).** With the hub enumerated and all four ports active, thermal-image U8. Expect ~272 mW; if the case runs hotter than ~40 °C above ambient the SOT-23-5 pour is inadequate and a larger package is needed.
10. **Isolation integrity.** Before any of the above: confirm > 100 MΩ between `GND1` and `GND2` with the board unpowered, and confirm no copper other than U1 and T1 bridges the barrier (visual plus continuity).
11. **DR-03 bulk capacitance.** Hot-plug a 500 mA load on each port and scope `ISO_5V` droop. If the dip breaks the 4.75 V floor, fit the additional 47 µF called for in DR-03.
12. **USB tiering sanity.** Enumerate through the isolator and confirm the hub reports 4 ports; remember the isolator consumes ~3 of USB's 5 tiers, so deeply cascaded hubs downstream are expected to fail (accepted spec limitation).
13. **DR-15 — upstream inrush (accepted).** `VBUS_HOST` carries ~20 µF of input capacitance (C3 + C7 + bypass) with no inrush limiting — about 2× the USB 2.0 §7.2.4.1 bus-powered limit of 10 µF/50 µC at hot-plug. Accepted for a prototype-class bench device (real hosts tolerate it); decide accept-vs-soft-start pad at layout.

## Final Verdict / Readiness

**Ready for PCB layout. Not yet ready to commit to fab.**

The schematic is electrically sound, internally consistent, and — for every IC in the design — verified against the manufacturer's own documentation with page citations that a gate re-checked against the PDFs. ERC is clean at error severity, the BOM is fully specified with in-stock parts, and the single functional defect found during this review (the TPS2121 CP2 / VCOMP lockout gap) has been fixed with a one-wire change and no new components.

What stands between here and fab:

1. **DR-01 — done on paper, characterize on the bench.** The spec's power budget has been corrected: the bus-powered ceiling is the host's 500 mA, giving ≈310 mA on `ISO_5V` and ≈75 mA for downstream devices. The magnetics are not the constraint and T1 does not change. Bring-up step 4 confirms the numbers; it is not a go/no-go gate.
2. **DR-02, DR-03, DR-05** — three layout-stage additions (a bleeder pad, a bulk cap, a copper pour), all cheap, all with pads that should be placed now rather than retrofitted.
3. **DR-06's conditional hunting risk** — no action now. Watch for it at bring-up step 2; the mitigation (CP2 pull-up re-derived from `EXT_5V`) is only warranted if the DC-DC proves marginal under a weak external brick.
4. **Test points** — add them during layout; the bring-up checklist above needs ten nets probeable.
5. **One verification gap to close on paper** — a human check of the custom RI-20-1 footprint against ADI's outline drawing. (The crystal tolerance/stability question the first pass raised is now resolved: ±10 ppm initial tolerance on both parts.)
6. **Re-run this review's EMC, thermal, PCB and gerber sections once the board is laid out.** An isolated 420 kHz push-pull converter crossing a safety barrier is precisely the case where schematic-only review runs out of road.
